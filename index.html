About us

Iruvé (pronounced ee-roo-way) Collective was founded by Vasundhara Shiva Kumar, Kavya Narayana and Sayli Khadilkar. After years of working independently and finding themselves returning to the same projects, collaboration became less a choice than a way of working.

Iruvé is the Kannada word for ant. Like a colony, the practice believes in quiet order: many small decisions coming together without a single author, shaped by patience, instinct and connection to form something whole.

Every space is approached as a composition, where material, light, proportion and detail exist in conversation with one another. The whole is never found in a single idea, but in the relationships between many.

Meet the founders

Vasundhara Shiva Kumar

Vasundhara completed her Bachelor of Architecture at M.S. Ramaiah Institute of Technology in 2014. She began her career working with notable practices including Collage Architecture Studio and Total Environment, and later joined Studio Rūh as an Associate. These experiences shaped her understanding of architecture and interiors, while gradually drawing her towards the intimacy and detail of designing spaces from within.

Over the years, she found herself increasingly drawn to the freedom of an independent practice, developing a more personal and instinctive approach to design.

Outside the studio, Vasundhara is a trained vocalist and a lifelong musician. When she isn’t designing, she can often be found on stage, performing and indulging her other great love – music.

Kavya Narayana

Kavya studied architecture at M.S. Ramaiah Institute of Technology, graduating in 2015. During her first year at an architecture practice, she worked on interior projects, sparking her interest in interiors. She went on to pursue a Master’s degree in Interior Design at the Savannah College of Art and Design in the U.S.

After returning to Bengaluru, she joined Studio Rūh, gaining experience across a range of interior projects. She later started her own practice and co-founded Iruvé Collective. Her approach is rooted in materiality, detail and quieter elements that allow each space to develop its own character. She creates spaces that feel warm, thoughtful and nuanced.

Away from work, Kavya is happiest with a sketchbook, drawing nature. She enjoys travelling, trying new experiences and finding good food wherever she goes.

Sayli Khadilkar

Sayli graduated with a Bachelor of Architecture from the University of Mumbai in 2018. With over eight years of experience, she has worked across design studios in Mumbai and Studio Rūh in Bangalore on a diverse range of projects.

Her approach is rooted in curiosity, materiality and a strong attention to detail. She is drawn to spaces that feel natural and effortless, yet distinct in character. Each project develops its own language, shaped by its context, the people who inhabit it and the stories that unfold along the way. After establishing her own practice, she went on to co-found Iruvé, continuing this approach through a more collaborative and personal design practice.

When she is not designing, Sayli enjoys reading, travelling and exploring new places, ideas and experiences, often finding inspiration in the nuances of everyday life.

Soft Steel

Workspace Interiors

A 1,250-square-foot residence in Bengaluru is reimagined as a calm, cohesive workplace without altering its existing structure. Designed for a team of twenty, the interiors introduce warmth and clarity through soft wood, muted tones, sage accents and considered lighting. Former domestic spaces are given new purpose through restrained interventions and bespoke joinery, allowing function to sit quietly within the architecture. The result is a grounded workplace that values comfort, materiality and quiet confidence over spectacle.

Turmeric & Terra

Hospitality Architecture & Interiors

An old Bengaluru home finds a new chapter as a restaurant, with its character and memories carefully carried forward. The intervention adds a new layer without erasing what came before; restored timber, familiar objects, honest materials and filtered light come together to create a space that feels lived-in rather than newly made. Rooted in continuity rather than nostalgia, the design allows the old and new to settle naturally into one another.

Pandan

Hospitality Interiors

The first Bengaluru outlet of Gelato Factory, designed within a compact 200 sq ft footprint where every gesture has to count. Microcement, sage-green terrazzo and custom stainless steel lights, wall-mounted tables and taborets keep the space light and tactile, while a disc-studded entrance door and a mural of sculptural hemispheres echo the rounded forms of gelato scoops. Oversized cylindrical lights over the counter add a bolder, playful note, and the green is an ode to the brand’s newest flavours, Pandan Coconut and Avocado Pistachio.

An Ode to Antiquity

Residential Interiors

In the heart of Basavanagudi, an 850-square-foot apartment finds a quiet balance between memory and modernity. Rooted in the rhythms of traditional Indian living, the home is shaped by lime plaster, IPS flooring, reclaimed timber, cane and natural textiles. Deep reds, earthenware and collected objects bring warmth and character, while flexible spaces adapt to the rhythms of everyday life. Rather than imposing a singular aesthetic, the design allows material, memory and the lives of its inhabitants to become part of the composition.

Ālaya

Residential Interiors

In Bengaluru, a 3,000-square-foot home unfolds through a considered interplay of material, texture and craft. Warm wood, lime plaster, stone and woven details create a restrained yet layered palette, while concealed doors and bespoke joinery allow functional elements to recede into the architecture. From intimate, cocooning spaces to generous openings that draw the garden inward, each room carries its own character while remaining part of a cohesive whole. The result is a contemporary home that feels warm, tactile and deeply attuned to everyday life.
